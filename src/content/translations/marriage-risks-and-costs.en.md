In business teaching, there is an idea called a “decapitation-style acquisition”: a large company acquires an excellent company in the same field to take its advantages for itself, then hollows it out until the target is left with debts and an empty shell.

<mark class="theme-highlight">When I first encountered this idea, my immediate reaction was: isn't this just marrying someone rich?</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">But it isn't the rich person who gets hollowed out. It's you.</mark>

As Xiaomei or Xiaoshuai, you are foolish but undeniably beautiful. You believe you can use your looks and your “intelligence” to network upward. Given a substantial injection of money, you will… live as your true self.

As a high-quality asset, you attract the interest of all kinds of capital. Just as your life is flourishing, an attractive foreign investor approaches you.

The other party is stronger than you, so you contribute all your beauty, talent, intelligence, and qualifications as your investment. They contribute in return, promising to buy you anything and make you happy for life. <mark class="theme-highlight">Of course, whether the person is a man or a woman, these are only words… We all know such a promise is impossible in a relationship like this. Even I would never casually make promises to anyone…</mark> And I believe you should know that someone who readily promises you everything after knowing you for only a few days is probably not someone to entrust your life to…

You know the other party is a famous, popular, much-sought-after source of capital, so a fifty-fifty investment is unlikely. You step back and accept sixty-forty, or even seventy-thirty, giving them complete management and operational control of the joint venture—in other words, the power to determine where the marriage goes.

Beauty loses value, youth passes, and qualifications depreciate. You know that unless you commit another ten or twenty years to becoming an expert in your field, what currently looks like your best path is to move upward and secure a big outcome.

You are gambling too. You bet that they have more money than you, fewer brains than you, and more sincerity than you.

So you accept, and the two of you decide to marry.

Marriage is the biggest acquisition of your life, and the marriage agreement is the acquisition agreement. Beauty, qualifications, intelligence—everything is put on the table.

Romantic relationships also follow the principle of communicating vessels. Two people who appear vastly unequal can still be matched in value within a relationship. If you remain doubtful, you may find an explanation at the end.)

After marriage, your personal brand must be available to the joint venture for free, and your factory must produce both parties' products.

<mark class="theme-highlight">In other words, you go from Professor So-and-so or Manager So-and-so to somebody's husband or wife. Once you have children, you also become somebody's mother or father.</mark>

Your expertise and abilities are all put under someone else's name. Only your parents still remember your own.

Your time and energy are split in two, and you have to look after the family. After marriage, your social circle must also be pared down. Apart from valuable experts and elites, your hometown friends, primary-school teachers, and cosplay-circle friends must all be laid off, reassigned, or put into early retirement, like state-enterprise workers in 1992.

<mark class="theme-highlight">This is the reality behind the phrase “You're married now; settle down.”</mark>

Ideally, wealth and connections would be sexually transmitted after marriage. Responsibility for the family would theoretically be divided equally. Whether you contribute money, energy, or intelligence, once you are married with the certificate in hand, in business terms you have formed a joint venture.

So what is the shortest time needed to hollow out a joint venture neatly and respectably?

The answer is three years.

The acquisition agreement says annual production capacity is divided equally: half for the family and half for yourself.

Fine. In year one, the foreign investor produces 51% for you and 49% for themselves. This is the honeymoon period, and it passes peacefully.

Then in year two, they give you 49% and take 51%, saying the market is volatile.

You think: we're a family anyway, and it's only a 1% share. Wouldn't it look petty to argue? Whatever.

But things become increasingly wrong.

<mark class="theme-highlight">The promised advertising funds never materialize. Instead, your partner keeps putting you down in daily life, pushing you into the background to care for the household and children—after all, you don't earn as much as they do.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">At the same time, they reduce production of your products. Further education or promotion that would take time away from housework is forbidden, with family responsibility used to keep you down.</mark>

They also take over your original sales channels, turning your connections into theirs and your friends into their friends.

A distributor who wants your goods must buy theirs too: three cases of their goods to get one case of yours.

If someone invites you out, they must invite your partner too. Anyone wanting to work with you must go through them first.

Over time, your sales channels shrink, your core competitiveness disappears, and friction grows between you and your friends. You become increasingly unable to leave, while leaving you becomes less costly for them.

Moreover, marriage specifies how to do well but does not specify the penalties for doing badly. If the other person performs their obligations halfheartedly, there is nothing you can do.

<mark class="theme-highlight">Your partner staying away from home, not loving you, having an incompatible personality, or subjecting you to emotional neglect will not be written on paper, but these are things you may actually suffer.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">Then you wait to be drained, strangled, and starved, watching the other person grow new flowers on your corpse.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">When you want a divorce, you get nothing.</mark>

You cannot recover the brand you spent years building, because they only call you “Mrs. X.” You are worse off than before marriage, and nobody remembers that you were exceptional in your own right.

They also shut you out within their circle and your shared circle, preventing you for several years from using your own name or brand—once yours, now belonging to the joint venture—and from working in the same field.

<mark class="theme-highlight">They take all the connections you built together. The equivalent is being married with children and unable to find work in your field because you have already missed the best years for advancing your career.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">At that point, you can only place yourself in your friends' care, taking an insignificant little job outside your field in a friend's small company.</mark>

You need to leave yourself some time to build a portfolio and regain your feel for the work, don't you?

<mark class="theme-highlight">You spend the remaining half of your life in litigation. Even if you recover something, it is not a fair exchange for your brilliant youth.</mark>
