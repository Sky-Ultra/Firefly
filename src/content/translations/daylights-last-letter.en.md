Isn't love a strange thing?

It makes one person brave,

and another afraid.

Perhaps it makes a girl check her phone again and again at night,

waiting for a message that never seems to come.

It also makes a boy stand by the window late at night,

suddenly remembering her on the balcony of the school building,

her hair lifted gently by the wind.

Yet neither of them knows

that the other might be thinking of them too.

I once visited Dover in England.

There is a stretch of tall white cliffs there,

overlooking the English Channel.

When the weather is clear,

you can look far out across the sea,

even toward France.

The British call them the White Cliffs.

But I have always felt

they should have another name.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I want to call them “Daylight's Last Letter.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Someone once told me

that long ago,

if two people in love stood on the White Cliffs to say goodbye,

they must not look back.

Because the moment they looked back,

they would take their reluctance to part home with them.

And so many people stood there,

one leaving by boat,

the other remaining on the shore.

The sea wind scattered their last words,

but their tears remained on the white rock face.

One drop, then two.

Later, those tears dried

and became white lime.

Layer upon layer,

and so the White Cliffs grew taller.

No one knows, after all these years,

how many people's goodbyes the White Cliffs have borne.

And no one knows

how much of that whiteness comes from seawater,

and how much from a girl's unspoken plea to stay.

I think there should be rows of benches there.

No flowers are needed,

nor the sound of bells.

Those who could not stay together to the end

could sit there for a while,

without saying anything.

Just let the sea breeze gently brush their wounded hearts.

Back then, they parted from one another,

and the waves beneath the White Cliffs became an excuse for goodbye.

But I believe

that everyone who comes to the White Cliffs

should bring a bouquet of flowers and a ring.

They should stand there,

looking at those white rocks,

and let the cliffs remember their vows of love,

let the cliffs witness their eternal, true love.

Let daylight's last letter become a wedding invitation,

and let the world remember these White Cliffs.

And so the White Cliffs truly remembered.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Then, at least,

there is still one place in this world that knows

we once loved someone so earnestly…
