Toward somewhere far away

— Epigraph

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

“Do you know what the saddest kind of crush looks like?”

“People often say the hardest part is keeping your feelings to yourself and never finding the courage to say them aloud.”

“It’s watching from a distance.”

“It’s brushing past each other.”

“Some people even say that if you find the courage to let the other person know, whatever happens, at least you won’t be left with regrets.”

“But you know what?”

“I think that’s just how people make crushes sound simpler than they are.”

“To me, the real regret was never that you didn’t get a chance to speak.”

“It’s that you laid out everything you felt.”

“She saw every bit of it.”

“And then nothing happened…”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

My friend asked,

“If she already knows, is it even a secret crush?”

I said,

“First tell me what you think it means to like someone openly.”

He said,

“You like her without hiding it, you approach her in the open, and the whole world knows you want to be with her.”

I asked,

“What if she knows how you feel, but she hasn’t fully accepted it and is unwilling to let things go any further?”

He hesitated.

“So she didn’t accept it?”

“Right.”

“But you’re still in touch. You haven’t had a falling-out. There’s even still that ease between you that comes from knowing each other for a long time.”

I gave a little smile.

“That’s what wears you down.”

<mark class="theme-highlight">“Your feelings are already as plain as if you’d declared them openly.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“But you have to tread even more carefully than when they were a secret.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

He didn’t say anything.

I went on,

<mark class="theme-highlight">“When your feelings really are a secret, at least you can stay in the shadows and hold on to a hope that isn’t very realistic.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“You can tell yourself maybe she just doesn’t know, and maybe the story would be different if she found out one day.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“But when she’s known all along and still hasn’t moved an inch,”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“you lose even the room to fool yourself.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

My friend frowned.

“Why doesn’t she just make it perfectly clear?”

I said,

“Because we’ve known each other for a while.”

“We’d already spent a fair amount of time together before I fell for her.”

“She’s not the kind of person who would hurt someone by being cold.”

“She’s just drawn an invisible line, with a kind of polite reserve that keeps her neither too close nor too far away.”

“You can talk to her from your side of that line.”

“You can share bits of your day, exchange a few words now and then, even have what looks like a perfectly decent conversation.”

“But the moment you try to take half a step across it,”

“the moment you try to take the relationship a little deeper,”

“you feel the air go still.”

I paused.

“She doesn’t pick up what you’ve said, or she gently changes the subject.”

“It’s like reaching out to push a half-open door because you think there’s light inside, only to find it’s been locked from the other side the moment your hand touches it.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

My friend looked at me.

“What if you’re reading it wrong? What if she just hasn’t made up her mind?”

I shook my head.

“Quite the opposite.”

“The most honest thing about us is our instinct, even when our words are full of excuses.”

<mark class="theme-highlight">“She doesn’t have to spell out whether she likes you.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“You can feel it for yourself.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“You send her something with your whole heart in it, and it always lands lightly with her.”</mark>

“You drop a hint ever so carefully, and she never follows it up.”

“You think knowing each other this long must have brought you a little closer. Then you look back and realize you’ve been the only one moving forward.”

“She’s still where she started, without having taken a step.”

<mark class="theme-highlight">“It’s hard to hide that you like someone.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“It’s even harder to hide that you don’t.”</mark>

“It’s just that when you’re the one in it, you need to check over and over before you’ll admit what’s been in front of you all along.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

My friend was quiet for a long time.

“If you knew that early she didn’t like you, why didn’t you stop sooner?”

I said,

<mark class="theme-highlight">“When you love someone you can’t be with, the thing you get best at is finding a gentle explanation for every disappointment.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“You take her politeness for attachment.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“You take her silence for hesitation.”</mark>

“You take the fact that she hasn’t pushed you away entirely as a sign that things might still turn around.”

“You take time that’s long since stood still and tell yourself you’re slowly getting closer.”

“You explain away every hurt and disappointment by saying, ‘I’ll wait and see.’”

<mark class="theme-highlight">“As if being earnest and patient enough could get you an answer that was never yours to begin with.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

My friend sighed.

“That sounds cruel.”

I said,

<mark class="theme-highlight">“What’s crueler is that she hasn’t actually done anything wrong.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“If she were stringing you along, using you or hurting you, you could walk away with your head held high. You could even hate her.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“It’s actually easy to move on when you hate someone.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“What makes you feel so helpless is that she’s still the wonderful girl you knew.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“She just happens not to like you back.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“You can’t even find a reason to blame her.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“You can only watch all your earnest feeling turn into a quiet performance with one person onstage.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Later my friend asked,

“Do you regret meeting her, or letting her know how you felt?”

I said,

<mark class="theme-highlight">“I’d be lying if I said it didn’t hurt.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“Sometimes late at night, when everything is quiet, I wish I’d held back a little more.”</mark>

“If I’d swallowed my feelings, or just never fallen for her to begin with, would I have fewer regrets now?”

“But then I think that was impossible.”

<mark class="theme-highlight">“After knowing her this long, liking her was never something my reason could switch off.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“The way my heart raced was real. Wanting to be close to her was real. Wanting a future with her was real, too.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“It’s just that fate seems to like playing this kind of joke.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“It lets you meet someone you want to keep in your life and gives you enough time to grow attached.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“Then it gives you no place beside her for the road ahead.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“It lays all the groundwork, then stops dead on the page that matters most.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

My friend asked,

<mark class="theme-highlight">“Wouldn’t that leave you with nothing but regret?”</mark>

I said,

<mark class="theme-highlight">“Yes.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“That’s what unrequited love really looks like.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“It’s nothing like a great, tragic parting in a story.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“It’s an ordinary evening when you look at a conversation that stopped and never went any further, and it suddenly hits you.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“Your story had already reached the end, long before you knew it.”</mark>

“There wasn’t even a proper goodbye.”

Something suddenly occurred to me.

What scares us most has never been a clear rejection.

It’s offering someone your heart and having them look at it without throwing it away or accepting it.

They just set it gently at the corner of the table.

And the real question was never,

“Am I still not good enough?”

It was,

<mark class="theme-highlight">“When you’ve given her every bit of your sincerity and her heart still doesn’t stir at all, can you admit that there really is no future for the two of you?”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">If the answer is yes,</mark>

all that reluctance to let go will eventually turn into a very quiet sigh.

And this might be the cruelest truth about a secret crush that looks like an open one:

‘You held your love so high and made it so bright, afraid she wouldn’t see it. In the end, all it lit up was the shadow of you standing there alone.’

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

So in the end I understood.

In some relationships, the saddest possibility is rarely that you never met.

It’s that you wanted so badly to be sure of one thing:

you take a step toward her, and she’s willing to walk toward you too.

You open your heart, and she’s willing to come closer.

She fills your field of vision, and there happens to be a place for you in hers.

<mark class="theme-highlight">But if that person never walks toward you…</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

you discover

the deepest regret has little to do with asking for answers at the top of your lungs.

<mark class="theme-highlight">It’s feeling a hollow place in your heart and still putting away your disappointment with as much grace as you can,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">taking no further steps toward her and putting no more pressure on her.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">Then one day, time still carries you each somewhere different.</mark>

You don’t blame her.

She doesn’t owe you anything.

Just that at some point later on, something still aches quietly inside you:

some people don’t come into your life to walk with you to the end.

They let you find out how quiet and how sad it can be to give away your heart and get it back untouched.

<mark class="theme-highlight">Maybe this is the longest question unrequited love leaves anyone who has liked someone in earnest:</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">When the other person will never answer your feelings, how long does it take to come to terms with the version of yourself who hoped so much and still came away empty-handed?</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">I don’t know the answer, and I don’t want to come up with another reason to make myself feel better. As I said at the start, we’ve known each other for a while, and I’ve liked her all along. There’s no arguing with that.</mark>

Well, in any case, however I put it,

<mark class="theme-highlight">I’ve liked her in earnest</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">even if she doesn’t like me,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">even if this relationship can only go this far…</mark>

We can’t go back to how untroubled things were when we first met, and we can’t go forward to the future I hoped for

In the end, all that remains of this very visible secret crush is my own trouble making peace with it…

<mark class="theme-highlight">Let out a sigh. That’s how it is.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">Let the affection you can’t ask for go with the wind</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">To the girl I liked so sincerely, may the road ahead be smooth wherever you go…!</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="3" style="--empty-paragraphs: 3" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Epilogue:

I meant what I said. This really happened. I just wrote it with a little more polish than it had in real life

At this point I don’t have much reason to hide it. This essay won’t use the highest level of protection. Anyone who visits the site can open it as usual

As I said in the essay, of course I have regrets. I still miss her

I’m not that clear-headed or good at explaining my sadness so neatly. I’m just sad, and I wish things had gone differently.

I don’t want to keep bothering her or spend any more of my own time on this

<mark class="theme-highlight">I’ve liked her all along. She knows how I feel, but she hasn’t fully accepted it and doesn’t want to go any deeper with me. I can tell very clearly that she doesn’t like me. It’s so sad and so helpless to be caught in between, knowing it isn’t going anywhere when you’re already in it with your whole heart.</mark>

I don’t know how long it will take me to get through all this sadness.

But maybe for now all I need to admit is that I loved her sincerely and she didn’t love me back. Maybe that’s enough…

Ah, that’s how it is.

I really like her. I just wish we could’ve gone further than this.
