If I were a tree,

then most of the people in my life

would be like cheap fertilizer.

Time turned the knob that set the gears in motion,

and battered, yet to no one's surprise,

I still became an adult right on time, not a second off.

The memories of my youth

are like a scattering of wounds,

mostly small and shallow, like splinters.

Only when they come to mind now and then

do they sting, with a hint of rebellion.

I recite Li Qingzhao's words without expression:

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

“Everything is the same, yet everyone has changed; all is over. Before I can speak, my tears begin to fall.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Every word suddenly breaks through the floodgates within me,

and the grief pours out.

Sometimes people take the easy way out,

leaving their fate to some elusive notion of destiny.

But the destiny that brought us together was spent in the moment we met,

and the rest of the story

needs our own effort to piece together a happy ending.

Every missed connection bears a fault of yours and mine.

And if we want to form bonds with others,

we must accept the risk of tears.

The strength I once pretended to have

is shattered by scenes from the past.

First we entrust a self still entirely unfamiliar to the other person,

then spend a long time

getting to know every little part of each other's lives.

Finally, because our paths through life are different,

we face a parting we cannot avoid.

How beautiful those scenes are,

and how cruel.

I briefly tasted the happiness of a rush of dopamine,

then spent half a lifetime longing for that moment.

I stare blankly at fallen leaves,

and weep beneath the streetlights,

dragged along by longing and reluctance to let go,

willing to remain stranded in days that will never return.

Because I know

I will never again meet someone so wonderful.

I was saved by meeting someone,

then broken by saying goodbye.

I walked beyond the radius of my life,

yet never escaped the cycle of the music ending and everyone leaving.

In the silent undercurrents of the passing years,

we can no longer measure the ocean-wide distance between us.

We give everything we have, stumbling onward,

yet in the end we cannot withstand the word “fate,”

or the frailty of the bonds between people,

and are utterly defeated.

From then on, across heaven and earth, in the vast sea of people,

no one can reshape my body and soul

or draw a smile from me.

Life really is a solitary journey forever about oneself,

only occasionally entangled with others.

If I were a tree,

I would wish for rings that could keep growing forever.
