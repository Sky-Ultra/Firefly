I am finally beginning to understand,

that the real problem is not with other people,

but hidden within myself.

<mark class="theme-highlight">Seeking outward, I cannot find what I seek;</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">seeking inward, life keeps renewing itself.</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I used to think I understood love,

and knew how to understand and respect others.

When I began to face myself honestly,

<mark class="theme-highlight">I realized that I, too, had helped drive a relationship toward its breaking point.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">Sometimes I thought my silence was acceptance,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">when it was really a fear of starting an argument.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">Sometimes I thought communication was the key to improving our relationship,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">when my own inner contradictions kept me from saying what was truly on my mind.</mark>

Certain conflicts,

I thought, were a necessary part of learning to be together,

when they were really the beginning of a departure.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">Sometimes I thought my insistence was a matter of principle,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">when it was really a projection of my fixations.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">I had been living in the illusion that “I think I am already good enough,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">and have already learned how to nurture an intimate relationship.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">Claiming to be empathetic and willing to grow,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">as though I were playing an ideal version of myself.</mark>

Only when I broke down one day,

did I discover that this “goodness”

was the very source of my pain.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Perhaps all of us, at certain moments,

are acting out the person we claim to be.

We say “I do not care,” when we care very much;

we say “I understand,” when we do not;

we say “I have let go,”

when we are still holding on tightly, refusing to release our grip.

<mark class="theme-highlight">We cover our real emotions with beautiful words,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">yet drift further and further from who we truly are.</mark>

A piercing question,

an emotional collapse late at night,

finally made me realize,

<mark class="theme-highlight">I was not as stable, mature, understanding, or capable of love as I had thought.</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">Growth is not a steady journey toward something better,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">but a repeated process of being torn apart, falling apart, and rebuilding.</mark>

It asks me to acknowledge my inner contradictions, soft heart, stubborn words,

weakness, dependence, and avoidance.

The sides of myself I do not want to acknowledge

are precisely the parts I need to bring together.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I used to seek definite answers from other people.

I demanded responses from intimate relationships, lovers, and friends,

and even expected them to give me a sense of security.

Then I encountered many people and things that overturned my sense of what was right,

and gradually began to develop the thought

that “what they are doing must be right.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Perhaps it should be this way.

<mark class="theme-highlight">When I truly began to look within,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">I discovered that my past ideas about love and understanding</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">had been no more than a superficial imagining.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">I had mistaken dependence for deep affection,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">and dressed control up as care,</mark>

without realizing that a healthy relationship

is never about one person taking from or binding the other,

<mark class="theme-highlight">but about two independent souls nourishing each other.</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I began learning

to stop whenever I wanted to run away,

and ask myself, “What am I afraid of?”

Whenever I was about to say something I did not mean,

I would take a deep breath and express how I truly felt.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Acknowledging my inner contradictions no longer felt shameful,

but like loosening the shackles that had bound me for so long.

The moments that once broke me

have now become light illuminating the corners of my heart.

Slowly, I came to understand

that a sense of security is never a gift from someone else,

but a fortress I build with my own hands.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

When I stopped seeking approval from outside,

and stopped sustaining relationships through a performance of perfection,

I found myself more at ease with myself and with others.

There is still a long road of growth ahead,

and the tearing apart and rebuilding will happen again and again,

but I am no longer afraid.

Because I know

that every embrace of my true self

helps me become a more relaxed, freer person.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">But the starting point for every answer</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">can only be myself.</mark>
