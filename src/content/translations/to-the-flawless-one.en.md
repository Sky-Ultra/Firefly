If only that banquet could have ended a little later, I would have sat as far from the door as possible.

The ice on the table slowly melted, leaving a trail of water down the glass. You were still choosing compliments you liked, not quite satisfied with a vague answer, though not upset either. You tilted your head and asked again. At last, someone laughed because of you. Outside the window, the shadows of flowers moved across half the table. The ends of your pink hair rested in the thin dusk, as though spring had happened to stop here for a moment. I thought then that there was still time. We could talk about things that did not matter: a piece of clothing, a melody. Or we could say nothing at all and listen to you make even the silence feel lively.

I had so many things to tell you. Perhaps this was the only sort of moment in which I could have said them.

“From now on, stars will shine, because I have been here. From now on, flowers will bloom, because I have never left. Weave my arrows, my flowers, and my love into seeds of new life, and carry them to that withered land. Then let them blossom into the eternal, flawless flower of... humanity.”

But I was always too late. I followed those names, spoken of again and again, into the Elysian Realm, only to learn how many years the greeting beyond its door had been waiting. You showed no sign of having waited. You came to meet me lightly, as if you had only just finished picking flowers, with time left over to guess what was on your visitor's mind. All the solemn words I had prepared suddenly seemed useless. In front of you, even grief felt like something I should leave at the door, brushing the dust from my shoulders before coming in to sit.

At first, your smile bewildered me. I had seen too many people wear their suffering on their faces, and I imagined that anyone who had lived through that era must have quiet eyes. Yet you loved beauty and jokes. You knew perfectly well that people liked you, and never missed a chance to make them say it aloud. When you told the old stories hidden in sighs, they somehow contained wind, clear skies, and somebody's embarrassment at being caught off guard. I lingered over page after page of the past and forgot that I had come looking for an answer.

Perhaps that is where my reluctance to leave that realm took root. History is vast enough to hold the destruction of cities and the passing of ages, yet often has no room for a little idle pleasure. How someone laughs, what wine they like, whether they avert their eyes at a compliment: such things are deemed unimportant. But without them, how could I recognize someone I had never met after all those years? A conversation cut short still had more to say. People could sit together after an argument. Someone distant heard their name, hesitated, then turned back after all. Those were the things that made it hard for me to leave.

The pink crystal flowers still bloom in the realm.

Among the others, you always seemed a little busy. If someone said too little, you went to ask them a question; if someone shut themselves away for too long, you insisted on knocking. Gradually, the number thirteen grew warm to me. I no longer saw only distant names engraved in stone. I saw a long table with a few empty seats, where people of different temperaments, not always in agreement, had each left a cup of their own. Even the wind seemed to soften there for a while.

Only after learning the ending did I find myself distracted whenever I heard you laugh. I listened for a pause at the end of a sentence, trying to find some hesitation no one had heard. I also wondered, for no good reason, how someone who loved company so much passed an evening alone. Was the window open? Did a petal cling to your sleeve, and did you see it without brushing it away? The past cannot answer such questions. Yet I stubbornly imagined someone knocking then, if only to ask whether the lamp should be left on. Would that have made you feel a little better?

People call you the Flawless One. Each time I hear it, I want to set that title down somewhere lower, somewhere I can reach. I want to see you looking pleased with yourself, deliberately withholding a secret, or frowning at a word that is not quite lovely enough. I even hope that, now and then, you might not feel like understanding anyone. You could close the door and let everyone wait. Someone would tend the garden. You could sleep through the afternoon and wake at dusk without having missed anything important.

It is a small, selfish wish to say aloud. You have so many people to love, and all I can think is whether I might borrow one aimless afternoon from them.

There would be no need to promise anything beyond it. When the shade moved, we would move our chairs; when the wind turned the pages of a book, we would stop at the page it opened. You could go on talking about other people, about someone's latest amusing story or someone else's stubborn old habit. If you happened to ask me a question, I would think about it for a very long time. My answer should sound ordinary, not so eager that you saw through me, but not so ordinary that you forgot it at once. By the time I had decided, I would probably have missed my moment and would have to lift my glass and pretend I had been drinking the whole time.

But even an afternoon is a luxury. The crystal flowers you left behind will not wither, yet the people who came to admire them have long since gone. The more I learned of the secret you carried, the less I wanted to ask about your distant origins. How the stars move, how fate is written, why a life comes into this world: once those questions loomed so large before you that they almost hid you from view. Yet you had already come so far among us. Rain you had walked through fell on your shoulders; songs you had heard stayed in your heart. You stopped for another person's pain and leapt for joy at another person's happiness. If all of that still could not put your mind at ease, I do not know what answer would.

What hurts me most is that, even as you were leaving, you still needed to know whether you had finally become human.

How I wish someone had answered you sooner. Before the banquet began, before you had chosen your dress: soon enough that you could hear it, laugh at how seriously it was said, and go out into the day. Not every day that followed would need a purpose. You could waste a few, change your mind about a few things, leave behind some little tasks done imperfectly. Someone who is loved ought to be able to live like that. And you had loved others so patiently, looking twice even at the awkward parts of themselves they wanted to hide.

But the past did not change in the slightest because I wanted it to. The appointment that had to be kept was kept. The candle that had to burn away fell quiet at the very end.

You made your farewell too beautiful. So beautiful that sometimes I resent the flowers for blooming so lavishly just then, and the music for continuing to flow without faltering on a single note. I learned that the world need not darken when someone leaves it. A skirt can fall lightly, a smile can shine, and among flowers in full bloom there can still be a journey with no return. Looking at it all, I suddenly forgot what I had meant to say. The idle talk I had thought so plentiful a moment before now seemed too precious to lose a single word of it.

For a long time after I left the Elysian Realm, I could not accept certain sunny days. That lucid blue spread all the way to the horizon, as though no one had ever been missing. The flowers kept blooming, without a trace of apology. I knew it made no sense, but I thought you would have liked weather like this, and so there was something irreparably absent from it.

I once tried to find you in every beautiful thing. I took the wind for a letter and a flower opening at just the right moment for an answer. At first it was easy. Later I could no longer bear to do it. If every flower was you, where should I look when I truly wanted to remember you? I want to remember your voice, your preferences, all those little airs no one else could imitate. The world may have as many lovely springs as it likes, but I still carry an unspeakable regret: they are all beautiful, and I can no longer ask whether you like them.

As time passed, I gained a patience I had not had before. I became less willing to call someone dull without knowing them. Sometimes a blunt answer conceals kindness that took a long time to find its words; a face accustomed to silence can brighten suddenly when its owner speaks of something they love. In moments like that I am briefly startled. If you had been there, you would probably have noticed long ago. I took a much longer road before I learned to listen to a sentence I had nearly let pass. Even after hearing it through, I might not know how to answer well. I would simply be willing to sit a little longer.

Wherever your gaze once rested, I gradually began to notice colors I had never seen. Even the ordinary days I had grown tired of now seemed worth regretting if I hurried through them with my head down: warm clothes drying by a window, flecks of gold on the river at dusk, or a smile that finally reached someone's lips after they almost said something. I still cannot embrace it all as freely as you did. I can be cold, withdraw, or pull back a hand I had already extended. But perhaps next time I can reach a little farther.

When I think of that banquet now, I still wish it had ended later. That much has never changed. Yet outside the window there are new mornings and evenings, and there are many places I have yet to go. If we do meet again, I cannot tell you only about our parting. I will bring news from the road, a few little things I was ashamed to have done poorly but finally put right, or a flower whose name I do not know. If you ask, I will tell the story slowly, slowly enough for the ice in our glasses to melt and the daylight to fade inch by inch, with another sentence still to come.

As for that small thing I have kept in my heart so long, perhaps I still will not be able to explain it. Perhaps you understood it all along and were kind enough to let me keep my awkwardness. I only hope that, somewhere in our conversation, you will suddenly laugh and think that this person, strange and familiar at once, finally has a story worth hearing.

Then the shadows of the flowers outside will move across the table again. I will push the pages I have written toward you, and the wind will turn to the first one. What I left unwritten, or wrote and then crossed out, I will not mention unless you ask. But this one sentence I want you to see with your own eyes.

May stars shine upon the road ahead of you. May flowers bloom wherever you leave your footprints.

From here on, it is *your* story.

”With me it begins, and with me it ends“

Elysia…

This story exists because of you.
