<mark class="theme-highlight">⌈I⌋ once liked a girl that way, too.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">⌈She⌋ was always so lovely</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">⌈She⌋ won’t come my way…</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">But you know, one day we’ll wake from the dream, and the things we remember will keep shining.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">The same goes for ⌈her⌋.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">⌈She⌋ is different.</mark>

— Epigraph.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

“Do you know what Plato really meant?”

“A lot of people say Platonic love is all about the soul, with the body left out.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

“A love of the spirit.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

“A pure kind of love.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

“Some even think a love without physical intimacy is somehow superior to ordinary love.”

“But did you know?”

<mark class="theme-highlight">“That’s precisely where modern readers misunderstand Plato most.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“Plato never told you that true love meant doing away with the body.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

“He talked about love because he wanted to understand some trickier things.”

“Why do people love beauty?”

“How can someone’s presence make another person start asking who they are?”

“Why does falling in love suddenly make an ordinary person think about what is true, good and beautiful, about knowledge and even about truth itself?”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

My friend asked me,

“So what is Platonic love, really?”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I said,

“First tell me what you think it means to love someone.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

He said,

“You like her, you understand her, you want to be with her.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">I asked,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“What if she doesn’t want to be with you?”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

He looked taken aback.

“Then I’d let it go.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

“Why?”

“Because she doesn’t like me.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I laughed.

<mark class="theme-highlight">“So do you love her, or the way you feel when she likes you back?”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

He said nothing.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I asked again,

“If someone no longer lives up to the person you imagined, do you still love her?”

“Suppose she’s less gentle, stops putting your needs first, no longer replies right away or looks up to you, and even starts to become someone completely different from you.”

“Would you still call her your soulmate?”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

He was quiet for a long time.

“Probably not.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I said,

“That’s the problem.”

<mark class="theme-highlight">“What many people call a meeting of souls is really someone happening to fit the script they’ve written.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

“But isn’t Platonic love just spiritual love?”

my friend asked.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

“No. Or at least there’s more to it.”

<mark class="theme-highlight">“What Plato was really concerned with was how love could begin with being captivated by one person and gradually lead us to ask about beauty, the soul, knowledge and even truth.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

My friend frowned.

“That doesn’t sound like being in love.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I said,

“That’s because you have too narrow an idea of love.”

“You think love is two people holding each other.”

<mark class="theme-highlight">“What Plato wanted to ask was what happens to a person’s inner life once they fall in love with someone.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“At first it might be her face you fall for, then it’s her ideas that make you stay, and later you grow fond of the particular way she moves through life.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“Eventually, having met her might make you ask for the first time what beauty is, what goodness is, and what you really want out of life.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I paused.

“She may be where love begins.”

“But she’s opened a door for you.”

“There’s a world beyond it that’s greater than she is.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

My friend suddenly laughed.

“When you put it that way, Platonic love sounds pretty romantic.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I shook my head.

“Quite the opposite.”

“It’s actually much crueler.”

“It asks you to understand something people today find especially hard to accept.”

“What’s that?”

<mark class="theme-highlight">“Loving someone doesn’t mean you own them.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“You can love her deeply and she still belongs to herself.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“You can hope she stays, but you have to accept that she’s free to leave.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“You can want to get closer to her, but love gives you no right to decide who she should become.”</mark>

“That’s the part of love that’s truly difficult.”

“And yet plenty of people say they want Platonic love.”

“They don’t want physical intimacy.”

“That’s perfectly fine.”

“The problem is that some people mistake going without physical intimacy for being spiritually superior.”

“As though leaving the body out meant you automatically had a soul.”

“But someone can claim your feelings for themselves without ever touching your body.”

“They can talk about respecting your freedom while demanding to know why you haven’t replied.”

“They can say they respect boundaries and still get angry when you live differently from what they expect.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

“What do you call that?”

my friend asked.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I said,

“Possessiveness of the spirit.”

<mark class="theme-highlight">“People look down on bodily desire, yet rarely notice that controlling someone’s thoughts, feelings and choices can be more possessive than touching their body.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I said,

“True Platonic love can include desire.”

“You feel desire without treating it as something you’re entitled to.”

“You want her to stay, but you don’t use love as a rope to hold her back.”

<mark class="theme-highlight">“You want to change her, yet in the end you accept her right to become someone you may never understand.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

My friend asked,

“So is letting go the highest form of loving someone?”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

“No.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

“Then what is?”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“It’s when you don’t need to let go to prove how noble you are.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“If she stays, you respect her.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“If she leaves, you respect her, too.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“If she loves you, you treasure it.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“If she doesn’t love you, you accept it.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“Your feelings are yours. They don’t become her obligations.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I looked at him and said,

<mark class="theme-highlight">“True freedom was never about doing whatever I want.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“It’s about being able to want something without letting that desire make my decisions for me.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Later my friend asked,

“Then why do so many people misunderstand Plato?”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I said,

<mark class="theme-highlight">“Because people love giving their feelings names that sound more elevated.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“They can’t have it, so they call it fate.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“They can’t forget, so they call it devotion.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“They’re indifferent, so they call it seeing clearly.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“They’re avoiding something, so they call it having boundaries.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“They’re afraid to love, so they call it independence.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“They’re in love with a fantasy, so they call it a soulmate.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“Sometimes we do want to love. We’re just afraid of how real and particular a person can be.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“So we’d rather love an idea that can never disappoint us.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

My friend asked,

“So you think Platonic love is a lie?”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I said,

“No.”

“I just think genuine Platonic love is much rarer than we imagine.”

“It brings a person’s dignity back into love, with their body still a part of it.”

“It means letting the other person be different from you, instead of asking them to be just like you.”

<mark class="theme-highlight">“It means seeing her as she is and still wanting to understand her, instead of keeping her trapped in your imagination.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

He said,

<mark class="theme-highlight">“Then what if one day I find out the person I love is nothing like the person I imagined?”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I said,

<mark class="theme-highlight">“That’s when you really begin to love her.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

“Why?”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">“Because up to that point, you loved the person you imagined.”</mark>

“After that, you finally have a chance to meet her.”

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

A line suddenly came back to me,

<mark class="theme-highlight">“What people fall for most readily is the part of themselves that someone else happens to satisfy.”</mark>

Yet the real question was never,

“Is she my other half?”

It was,

<mark class="theme-highlight">“When she no longer matches my idea of her, am I still willing to recognize that she is a whole person?”</mark>

If the answer is no.

Then she was never the one you loved.

You only fell in love with an illusion that did as it was told.

And this may be the cruelest truth about “Platonic love”:

<mark class="theme-highlight">‘A true love of souls never makes two people into one. It is two whole people who can draw close to each other without ever losing the right to be themselves.’</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

So later I finally understood.

Maybe a “soulmate” was never what we truly longed for.

We just want so badly to meet someone who can

<mark class="theme-highlight">see right through us without trying to remake us,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">draw close without trying to claim us,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">and love us while allowing some parts of us to remain beyond their understanding.</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

And if there really is such a person…

you’ll find

the deepest love never turned you into someone’s other half.

It simply let you, beneath another person’s gaze,

see yourself whole for the first time.

Then one day, each of you goes your own way.

You don’t hate her.

And she owes you nothing.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

You simply realize all at once,

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">some people come into your life without ever being meant to be your destination.</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">They only let you discover that once one soul has been lit by another, the light doesn’t go out the moment that person leaves.</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">Maybe this is the question Plato left love to ponder the longest:</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">Are we loving a person, or are we slowly learning through loving one person how to love the world?</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">I don’t know the answer, and I don’t want to know. As I said at the start, I like her. There’s no arguing with that.</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Well, either way, however you put it,

<mark class="theme-highlight">I have loved the person I love</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">and whoever she goes on to love,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">I hope she’ll be happy…</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">We’re neither simply friends nor openly lovers</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">It was all just me wishing for something on my own…</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">Everyone has their own way of understanding love. Neither of us needs to be Plato. Go find the person you love who loves you back</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">Good luck to you, my beloved on the far horizon</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="3" style="--empty-paragraphs: 3" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Epilogue:

The ⌈I⌋ in this piece isn’t me. I don’t have all these forward-thinking ideas about the person I love. I just like her.

I don’t really understand what people call love. I still don’t know myself well enough to know what kind of relationship I want… But this thing so many people seek in Plato, this respect, this freedom, does speak to me. Whoever she becomes, that’s still her. I love more than a fantasy. I feel it deeply, but I won’t make her do anything. I want her to be happy, to find joy and to be herself.

I don’t know why I like her.

<mark class="theme-highlight">But maybe all I need to know is that love means two people together for a lifetime…</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="3" style="--empty-paragraphs: 3" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I l-like you!
