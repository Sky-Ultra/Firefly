After unpinning you and turning off special notifications for you, I found myself feeling unexpectedly lighter.

I no longer open our conversation the moment I pick up my phone, hoping for a message from you. I no longer count the seconds since we last spoke, or keep guessing what you might be doing. Giving up so many futile, exhausting habits has finally brought me a little peace.

The time of our last conversation retreats from a precise minute today to yesterday, then to a day of the week, until it settles forever on a date. The conversation I once kept pinned now sits among people I barely know and group chats long abandoned, sinking with them to the very bottom of the list.

A whole list of distant acquaintances, with the person I care about most hidden among them. Do you know what that feels like? As though, to put an end to my hopes, I had packed my most treasured diary with some unimportant old books into a dusty storage box, hiding all those pages of anticipation, excitement and joy in a corner no one knows about.

Yet I still secretly hope that you will appear of your own accord and leap to the top of the conversation list, so I will not have to search all the way through it to find you. Everyone else is muted; only you are the exception. When my phone makes a sound, I know it is you without looking. Your presence has never been a disturbance. But even if it were, you have not disturbed me in a long time.

Still, as long as I do not examine the past too closely or recall the little things, I can fool myself. I can convince myself that you are no longer someone I watch for so closely, that perhaps life will grow steadier once I stop expecting you. Out of sight, out of mind.

Perhaps, from that moment on, I was already preparing myself for you to leave.

Being prepared to leave you and preparing to leave you are, in fact, two different things. Deep down, I cannot bear to part with you. But I am even more afraid that a farewell will come before I am ready and catch me completely off guard. I still want to see you. I still want to talk with you about nothing in particular. I cannot let go of you completely, no matter what I do. And I cannot seem to stop hoping for you either.

Among all those avatars belonging to people I have not even given a contact name, there is one that, long ago, would fill me with delight the moment I saw it. It is still there. Every now and then I scroll through the list, and scroll, and scroll, until I find that familiar avatar. I look at it quietly for a long time, thinking of this and that, before switching off my phone.

Then one day, you suddenly said that you liked how easy and relaxed it felt to spend time with me. You had probably always thought of me as someone casual and untroubled. Your words were as gentle as ever, unchanged. They made it hard for me to move on, and almost made me abandon every defence I had prepared and fall into the same pattern again. But I understood: that ease you spoke of was precisely a sign that I had prepared myself to leave. Perhaps only by drawing in all my expectations, and accepting that a farewell could come at any moment, could I speak with you so peacefully.
