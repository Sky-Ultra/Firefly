This article offers only some preliminary thoughts on a social phenomenon. It does not refer to any particular person, nor does it represent my own circumstances in real life.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Are you sexually repressed?

“格调,” “福,” “超市,” “法,” “踩踩背,” “相册,” “学历,” “链接”...

If we had to choose the most prolific category of internet meme in 2026, it would undoubtedly be sexual jokes.

Let me add a disclaimer, as usual. This is not to say that sexual jokes are right. But at this point, they are certainly among the most common memes on websites and video platforms, particularly beneath certain kinds of videos.

<mark class="theme-highlight">People call out “Mom” in comment sections, ask “What if?”, or post “Finally, feet!” There is even the striking debut of Dongbei Yujie's “Great Northeast Universe”:</mark>

“Yujie has a certain allure. I really don't think those delicate little women can compare.”

“The way Little Yu holds back her voice is so cute.”

Many people say we have entered an “age of great repression,” meaning that everyone is sexually repressed. <mark class="theme-highlight">There is even a Mr. “Freud Feng,” supposedly the internet's foremost expert on sexual repression, who declares</mark>: sexual repression is the root of every problem.

“There are so many. I can't say all of them, but 99% of psychological problems come from sexual repression.”

“You don't like white socks, or calves, or girls. You're sexually repressed.”

“Feng Ge, there's a saying that repressed men are best matched with repressed women. She's repressed, and so are you. How do you tell whether a woman is repressed?”

“Uh, as long as she's a woman, she's repressed...”

“A conversation shared by several people, a few words of goodnight, stirring affectionate daydreams, yet scattering so easily.”

<mark class="theme-highlight">So here is the question: are we really as desperate as Feng Ge says? And why have we entered such an age of great repression?</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="3" style="--empty-paragraphs: 3" aria-hidden="true"></div>

First, let me ask you something: which age group do you think is the most repressed today?

In my view, it would be young people between sixteen and thirty.

What about those a little older? Most people over thirty have already started families and established careers. Their repression may have eased, or it may no longer be their foremost concern.

And a little younger? At fourteen or fifteen, ideas about these matters are still rather vague. Even when the first stirrings of affection appear, they do not necessarily produce particularly strong desires.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">But during young adulthood, especially while at secondary school or university, it can feel as though you are stuck somewhere between moving up and moving down.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">By then, one's understanding of these matters has often become relatively mature. Under the pressure of study and work, people deeply crave emotional comfort and intimacy with others. Yet school rules forbid early romance, or they cannot find a suitable partner, so they can only continue keeping that repression inside.</mark>

It is like having read every game guide and learned it all by heart, ready to put your skills into practice, only to discover that the system has assigned you no romance target—or that the game has shut down entirely.

That is why this age group has the greatest number of repressed people, and the deepest degree of repression.

Beyond the reasons already mentioned, there is another particularly important cause of repression during one's school years: the absence of sex education in China's education system.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Think back over your classes while growing up. Was there ever a systematic, comprehensive introduction to sexuality?

Even when biology lessons covered reproduction and development, the subject was usually passed over quickly without further explanation. Outside school, family education was generally even less likely to involve any discussion of sexuality at all.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

It is like when we used to ask our parents as children, “Where did I come from?”

The answer was often not a serious explanation of reproduction, but “We picked you up from a rubbish heap.”

Clearly, in both the family and school systems that shape young people, sex education has been absent or played down.

<mark class="theme-highlight">On one side, enormous exam pressure leaves students craving an emotional outlet. On the other, sex education is downplayed, while schools and families jointly forbid early romance just as affection begins to stir. Both ends of the same passage are blocked. If you aren't repressed, who would be?</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">The result is that, by university or working life, years of repression have already hard-coded sexual shame into one's mind. Even when interactions with the opposite sex are finally allowed, people do not know where to start, so the repression continues.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">This lack of sex education has also brought us a sense of shame about our bodies.</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="2" style="--empty-paragraphs: 2" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Many people attribute this repression to the remnants of thousands of years of feudal culture, sighing, “Ah, it's all because of our ancestors' wisdom.”

But in fact, our ancestors were not quite so repressed.

Silk manuscripts unearthed from the Western Han period include writings on the arts of the bedchamber, covering sexual knowledge, techniques, treatment of sexual dysfunction, conception, and more.

Erotic illustrations were also part of some women's dowries in the mid-Ming period. Their content was direct and explicit, but their function was sex education.

The famous *Jin Ping Mei*, which many people today feel ashamed to discuss, circulated openly during the Wanli era. Scholars passed it among themselves; it was not a banned book.

The celebrated *Book of Songs*, compiled during the Spring and Autumn period, also contains a great deal about romantic and sexual desire in its “Airs of the States” section.

Our ancestors, then, were relatively open about these matters rather than uniformly repressed. The idea that sexual repression has been an inherent Chinese trait for thousands of years does not hold. Do not simply blame the ancients.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">This modern sexual repression has, in fact, been constructed layer upon layer by modern society.</mark> Part of it comes from the lack of sex education discussed earlier. But a deeper cause may be not merely our desire for sexual satisfaction, but our pursuit of love and affection.

<mark class="theme-highlight">In plain language, it isn't as simple as wanting to “hook up.” We want to be loved.</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Let us return to sexual memes.

A defining event in the emergence of this age of great repression was the internet's collective obsession with Dongbei Yujie.

In 2024, following Dongbei Yujie's scandal, a wave of declarations of love for her swept the internet. At first, this frenzy may have been little more than following a trend of mockery, creating satire through jokes and incongruity.

But by last year, this wave had not subsided; it had grown stronger. By then, what the fixation on Yujie represented had undergone a change in nature.

For a while, comment sections both related and unrelated to Yujie were filled with declarations that looked ironic, but actually used irony to express real feelings:

“I've imagined myself as Lao Kuai countless times.”

“Yujie is every northeastern boy's dream of adulthood.”

There were even people making sexual jokes about her.

A key reason that Yujie—someone usually seen as far removed from conventional sexiness—could become the subject of sexual memes is that everyone knows it is a joke. Nobody is seriously proposing a relationship with her.

<mark class="theme-highlight">Yujie can therefore serve as a harmless object of emotional projection, allowing me to pour out affection freely without moral condemnation.</mark> If those same words were directed at someone in real life, they would probably have been condemned as harassment long ago.

<mark class="theme-highlight">Likewise, memes such as “格调,” “福,” and “链接” tend to appear beneath absurdist videos and rarely under serious ones. People are using absurd humour to express emotional repression safely.</mark>

We also often see people shouting “Mom” in comments or bullet chats. At first, this was associated with younger *Valorant* players calling women “Mom.” It gradually spread to every corner of the internet, until people were calling female streamers of all kinds, in all categories, “Mom.”

Whether someone enjoys posting sexual memes and acting absurd beneath surreal videos, or likes calling out “Mom,” the underlying logic is the same:

<mark class="theme-highlight">I need an emotional outlet that will accept me unconditionally, without exposing me to risk.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">Unconditional acceptance and an absolute sense of safety: these ideas resemble a pursuit of love more than a simple desire to “hook up.”</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">Feng Ge says every problem comes from sexual repression. I would add this: all sexual repression, in fact, comes from the repression of love.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">I do not deny the existence of sexual desire, but desire and repression are different things. Desire seeks physical satisfaction; repression is often more closely tied to a longing for emotional resonance.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">The lack of sex education in modern society has created our shame about discussing sexuality. Meanwhile, the suppression and prohibition of early romance have also prevented us from learning how to love when those feelings first emerge.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">Thus, our exploration of both sex and love lacks guidance in modern society. These subjects have been left blank. That is why so many people feel embarrassed to discuss sex and afraid to express love.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">The two reinforce each other's repression, ultimately creating the distorted condition we call sexual repression.</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I once came across a post in which someone wrote:

“I am sexually repressed, and I feel ashamed. My adolescence was accompanied by repression. Because I could not find what I needed at home, my longing reached its height during puberty.

<mark class="theme-highlight">“A few kind words can make me regard someone as irreplaceable. I can pour all my imagination into a future as insubstantial as a soap bubble. I can cry for days over someone on the other side of a screen. I can spend half a year dejected over the loss of a brief relationship.”</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">And am I really so different myself... A lack of companionship, a lack of love, have also made me who I am today...</mark>

Many people face this predicament. The absence of education about these matters ultimately leaves them hurt in real intimate relationships—hurt that could have been avoided.

Society and families have not taught us how to navigate sex and love, two of the most basic subjects of being human.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Modern society expects our lives to be prefabricated: follow the school's order as a student, follow the company's order as an employee, then after graduation find another person with an equally prefabricated life, form a new prefabricated life together, and have a prefabricated son or daughter. The cycle repeats.

Within that process, there is supposedly no need to explore sex and love, so there is no corresponding education or public understanding. That is part of modern society's cruelty and sadness.

What I want to tell you is this: before becoming someone covered in labels of worldly success, perhaps you must first become a person.

<mark class="theme-highlight">You may struggle with sexual repression or with a lack of love, and society should make room for those struggles. You can give yourself time to explore love. Society's standards should allow that space, rather than alienating sex and love through pressure to marry, pressure to have children, gender conflict, and the matchmaking market.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">As for me, I will never turn to matchmaking or seek someone I do not like. In fact, I do not believe affection can simply be cultivated slowly. Love can be performed, but a secret crush cannot.</mark> Even after writing all this, I still do not know whether I truly understand the subject. If I really cannot find my pure love, if I never get the chance to find my Idrila, perhaps I will remain repressed, keeping it all inside...

Sex is not shameful, and love is precious. You can allow yourself, for a while, to set aside the external pressures that repress you.

In that sense, those younger *Valorant* players are undoubtedly brave. At least they dare to call the woman gaming with them on the other side of the computer “Mom.” In their own way, they confront their repression.

And how many people have grown up with such social anxiety that they cannot manage even that?

When people can share sexual jokes and call out “Mom” in appropriate settings, within reasonable boundaries, rather than venting indiscriminately or harassing others through private messages, it also shows that they have learned to regulate and express their desires in an orderly way.

That is itself a small resistance against this age of great repression. I would call it a healthy, sustainable cyber orgy.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Only by gradually breaking down sexual shame and taboo, allowing sexuality to become something people of the same or different genders can discuss openly rather than a form of harassment or violence, might we finally pursue and possess the right to love.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

May the world no longer be so repressed, and may everyone find their own ⌈Beauty⌋.
