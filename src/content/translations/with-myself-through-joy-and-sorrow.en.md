I am always like this.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I always think that if only I run fast enough,

hold my sincerity high enough,

I will be able to see, in the mirror of another soul,

the shape of my own existence.

I try with all my strength to resonate, to interpret,

to carve, into every ambiguous detail,

proof of destiny.

<mark class="theme-highlight">I built a grand illusion of mutual affection,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">and lost myself completely</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">in a one-person play performed by me alone.</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Until one day,

the bonfire I lit with my own hands

was extinguished by a casual reply.

I stood there, stunned, and finally understood:

<mark class="theme-highlight">the mirror had never existed.</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">All the light and warmth I had felt</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">were merely my sincerity reflected off a cold wall.</mark>

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I once thought I was moving toward someone, toward an answer.

Only later did I realize

I was walking toward the deepest battlefield within myself.

With my most ardent sincerity as fuel,

I lit a lamp.

Not to illuminate its outline,

but to see where I myself stood.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

They say I take things too seriously.

But I know that without that earnestness, I would not be me.

My pain has never come from taking things seriously,

but from what follows:

expecting another heart to resonate with an equal echo.

That is the real disappointment.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

I finally learned

the cruelest and gentlest gesture:

to withdraw.

I discovered

<mark class="theme-highlight">that the self who needed to be saved, acknowledged,</mark>

<mark class="theme-highlight">and remembered deeply had already gone far away.</mark>

The person standing now in this quiet place

is someone who can tell himself, “Enough,”

then turn and walk into the wind and rain without looking back.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

My strength

has never revealed itself in holding on tightly.

It comes in the moment I loosen my grip,

let the sand I cannot hold slip through my fingers,

and discover that my hands are still strong—

and, at last, free

to take hold of my own destiny.

<div class="word-paragraph-gap" data-empty-paragraphs="1" style="--empty-paragraphs: 1" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<mark class="theme-highlight">From now on, I sail with myself, through joy and sorrow.</mark>
