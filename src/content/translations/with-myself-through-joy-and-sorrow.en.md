You are always like this.

You always think that if only you run fast enough,  
hold your sincerity high enough,  
you will be able to see, in the mirror of another soul,  
the shape of your own existence.  
You try with all your strength to resonate, to interpret,  
to carve, into every ambiguous detail,  
proof of destiny.  
<mark class="theme-highlight">You built a grand illusion of mutual affection,</mark>  
<mark class="theme-highlight">and lost yourself completely</mark>  
<mark class="theme-highlight">in a one-person play performed by you alone.</mark>

Until one day,  
the bonfire you lit with your own hands  
was extinguished by a casual reply.  
You stood there, stunned, and finally understood:  
<mark class="theme-highlight">the mirror had never existed.</mark>  
<mark class="theme-highlight">All the light and warmth you had felt</mark>  
<mark class="theme-highlight">were merely your sincerity reflected off a cold wall.</mark>

You once thought you were moving toward someone, toward an answer.  
Only later did you realize  
you were walking toward the deepest battlefield within yourself.  
With your most ardent sincerity as fuel,  
you lit a lamp.  
Not to illuminate its outline,  
but to see where you yourself stood.

They say you take things too seriously.  
But you know that without that earnestness, you would not be you.  
Your pain has never come from taking things seriously,  
but from what follows:  
expecting another heart to resonate with an equal echo.  
That is the real disappointment.

You finally learned  
the cruelest and gentlest gesture:  
to withdraw.  
You discovered  
<mark class="theme-highlight">that the self who needed to be saved, acknowledged,</mark>  
<mark class="theme-highlight">and remembered deeply had already gone far away.</mark>  
The person standing now in this quiet place  
is someone who can tell himself, “Enough,”  
then turn and walk into the wind and rain without looking back.

Your strength  
has never revealed itself in holding on tightly.  
It comes in the moment you loosen your grip,  
let the sand you cannot hold slip through your fingers,  
and discover that your hands are still strong—  
and, at last, free  
to take hold of your own destiny.

<mark class="theme-highlight">From now on, you sail with yourself, through joy and sorrow.</mark>
